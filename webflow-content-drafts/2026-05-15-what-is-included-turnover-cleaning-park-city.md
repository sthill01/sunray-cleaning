## Intro (direct answer)

In Park City, turnover cleaning is the difference between “technically clean” and **guest-ready** — the kind of reset that protects your reviews and makes the property feel like the first guest of the season just walked in.

So what’s included in vacation rental turnover cleaning?

In most cases, a professional turnover clean includes:

- bathrooms (sanitized + spotless details)
- kitchen surfaces and high-touch areas
- bedrooms and living areas (presentation + dust + floors)
- trash removal and basic reset tasks
- a final walk-through (so small misses don’t become bad reviews)

Below is a practical breakdown you can use to compare cleaners, set expectations, and create a consistent standard for your Airbnb or VRBO.

---

## What turnover cleaning usually includes (arrival-ready)

### Bathrooms (the trust zone)

Most turnovers include:

- toilet sanitized (seat, rim, base area, handle)
- sink/counter cleaned; hair removed
- mirror cleaned streak-free
- shower/tub cleaned (surface reset; heavy buildup may require deep turnover)
- floors vacuumed/mopped
- trash emptied

What guests notice most:

- hair, streaks, and water spots
- clean-smelling bathroom (no lingering trash odor)
- a toilet that looks “newly cleaned,” not “wiped down”

### Kitchen (the attention zone)

Most turnovers include:

- counters and backsplash sanitized
- sink and faucet cleaned
- appliance exteriors wiped (fridge/dishwasher/microwave fronts)
- inside microwave wiped if needed
- cabinet fronts wiped around handles
- floors vacuumed/mopped

What guests notice most:

- crumbs on counters
- fingerprints on cabinets/appliances
- sticky handles

### Bedrooms + living areas (first-impression zone)

Most turnovers include:

- dust obvious surfaces (nightstands, tables, lamps)
- straighten and reset the space
- vacuum upholstery and visible crumbs
- floors vacuumed/mopped

If linens are included, it also includes:

- stripping/remaking beds (per your standard)
- staging towels (per your standard)

## Common add-ons (ask about these upfront)

Turnover cleaning can mean very different things depending on the property and your expectations. These are common add-ons:

- linen service and laundry (on-site laundry vs off-site process)
- restocking consumables (toiletries, paper products, coffee, etc.)
- photo documentation after cleaning
- inside fridge/oven (as needed)
- heavy glass (large windows, sliding doors)
- hot tub wipe-down / patio reset (property dependent)

If you’re comparing cleaners, ask whether these are included, optional, or excluded.

## “Standard turnover” vs “deep turnover” (the simple rule)

Use this rule of thumb:

- **Standard turnover** = the property is maintained and needs an arrival-ready reset.
- **Deep turnover** = the property has buildup, grime, or missed details that will show up in reviews (baseboards, vents, heavy glass, sticky cabinets, shower buildup, etc.).

Many Park City rentals do standard turnovers most of the time and schedule a deep turnover:

- at season transitions (pre-ski, post-ski, pre-summer)
- after a stretch of heavy bookings
- anytime the home starts feeling “tired” even when it’s cleaned

## FAQs

### Is turnover cleaning the same as a deep clean?

Not usually. Turnovers focus on arrival-ready reset between guests. Deep cleans add detail work and buildup removal and are often done seasonally or on a cadence.

### Do turnover cleaners restock supplies?

Sometimes. Restocking is typically a defined add-on or part of a property-manager scope. The best practice is to maintain a restock list and storage location instructions.

### Do I need a turnover checklist?

Yes. A checklist is the fastest way to reduce misses, keep standards consistent, and protect reviews — especially if more than one person cleans the property.

### How do I know if I need a “deep turnover”?

If showers show buildup, baseboards look dusty, cabinets feel sticky, or guests mention “not as clean as expected,” it’s time for a deeper reset.
